Better Vegetarian Protein Foods Than Eggs (Key Facts Only)
Egg
1 egg provides ~6 g protein.
Commercial eggs may have lower nutritional value if hens are raised in poor conditions.
Hens are often given antibiotics, which may contribute to antibiotic resistance.
Some studies cited in the video claim that eating more than 1 egg daily increases diabetes risk by 23%.
1. Urad Dal (Black Gram)
1 bowl (katori) provides ~15 g protein.
Rich in Magnesium, Manganese, and Zinc.
Magnesium helps muscle relaxation.
Manganese supports body strength.
Zinc supports testosterone production.
Considered muscle-building in Ayurveda ("Maash").
Adding hing (asafoetida) and bay leaf (tej patta) improves digestibility.
Can be substituted with green moong dal, which provides ~14 g protein.
Lentils are associated with a 20% lower risk of diabetes (as cited in the video).
2. Bhuna Chana (Roasted Chickpeas)
100 g provides ~22 g protein.
Roasting reduces anti-nutrients, improving protein absorption.
Protein quality is considered superior to many other plant proteins (as cited in the video).
Consume with the skin for maximum fibre.
Sattu (powdered roasted chana) provides ~7 g protein per scoop.
Also contains Iron, Calcium, Magnesium, and Folate.
Can be consumed as a drink, stuffing for parathas, or in cheelas.
Naturally low glycemic index.
3. Milk & Milk Products
1 glass of milk provides ~10 g quality protein.
Rich in Vitamin D, Vitamin B12, CLA, and Omega-3 fatty acids.
Drinking milk after workouts may:
Reduce muscle damage.
Improve muscle repair.
Increase strength.
Reduce muscle soreness.
100 g paneer provides ~20 g protein.
1 bowl of curd (dahi) provides ~7 g protein.
Ethically sourced milk products also contain nutrients that support lean muscle gain.
4. Green Peas
1 cup provides ~9 g protein.
Considered a complete protein containing all 9 essential amino acids.
Commonly used in vegan protein powders.
A study cited in the video reported pea protein performed similarly to whey protein for muscle growth.
May help reduce blood sugar levels.
Combines well with vegetables, pulao, or paneer.
One serving of matar paneer can provide ~25 g protein.
Ayurveda recommends consuming green peas preferably at lunch because they may cause gas.
5. Peanuts
One handful provides ~7 g protein.
Higher protein than almonds, cashews, or walnuts (according to the video).
Rich in healthy fats.
Peanut powder has been associated with increased muscle mass and reduced body fat (as cited in the video).
Can be eaten as a snack, added to poha, or made into peanut butter.
Regular peanut consumption is associated with a 13% lower risk of heart disease (as cited in the video).
Recommended intake: 15–20 peanuts or 2 tablespoons peanut butter per day.
Daily Protein Intake
Ayurveda emphasizes digestibility over protein quantity.
The video cites the China Study, recommending 5–10% of daily calories from protein depending on activity level.
An example 1800-calorie Indian diet (curd, aloo matar, dal, rice, milk) provides approximately 43 g protein, which the video states is sufficient for most people.
Individuals with higher fitness goals may require additional protein from these foods.
